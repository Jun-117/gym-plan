Weight rulesWhen to go lighter or heavier
  • After each set, you feel you could do 3–5 more → keep this weight.
  • Only 1–2 more → one step lighter for the next set.
  • 7 or more → week 1: just remember it. From week 2, if it happens twice → one step heavier.
  • One step = 1–2 kg dumbbell · 2.5 kg plate · one pin.
  • New machine → new weight. The same kg feels different on each machine. Find it with your first set.
  • Sharp joint pain → stop that exercise for today.
  • Machine shows lb? 10 kg ≈ 22 lb · 15 kg ≈ 33 lb · 20 kg ≈ 44 lb.
Stay safeStairs, feeling sick, when to stop
  • Stairs: you can talk, but you can't sing.
  • So you don't feel sick: a banana 30 min before · stairs start at level 1 for 1 min · walk slowly for 2–3 min after · sip water between sets.
  • Chest pain, a tight chest, or feeling like you might faint → stop right away.

Why this planThe reasons, in short
  • Mon & Thu = Hip Day — your main day. Tue & Fri = back, core and a little hip work, for your upper-body shape. Wed & Sun = cardio day.
  • Why so much cardio? ① You move a bit more instead of eating less — your food is already close to the lowest safe amount. ② Your hips get 48–72 hours to rest between Hip Days. ③ New lifters get very sore and quit — cardio days help avoid that.
  • Left out on purpose: squats, leg extension, calf raise, shoulder press, arm-only exercises. Squats work your bottom about as well as hip thrusts, but grow your thighs more (study: Plotkin 2023).
  • Chest: only 4 sets a week — on purpose.
  • First 3 weeks are easy — tendons get used to training more slowly than muscles.
  • Weeks 3–4: your legs may feel a bit bigger. That's water and stored energy in the muscles for a while, not bulk.
  • No incline while you wait for Jun — a steep 30-minute walk would burn more than your food plan allows (guess).
